How it works

Three steps. One of them is yours.

LeadRadar does the boring half of prospecting: the scrolling, the tab-opening, the squinting at ratings.

1

Tell it what you do

Your service and your city. That's the whole brief.

"Web development" · "Austin, TX"
2

It reads every business

It pulls live Google Maps listings, checks each one for a missing website or a shaky review history, and scores it 0–100.

No website found → score 85
3

It writes the first email

Your top ten leads each get a short, specific draft, signed with your name. Copy it, tweak it, send it.

Best, Alex
3 hrs
~1 min

A Monday morning of prospecting, gone. Scrolling Maps and reading listings one by one takes hours. A LeadRadar scan takes about a minute.

Features

Built for freelancers, not sales teams.

No CRM to configure, no seats to buy. Just a list of businesses worth emailing, and an email worth sending.

Real Google Maps data

Live listings for your city, not a stale directory someone scraped in 2019.

via SerpApi

Facts, not guesses

Signals like "no website" come straight from the listing. The AI never invents a problem the data doesn't show.

Checked in code

Emails signed by you

Short drafts built around the one thing LeadRadar found, ending with your name, not "[Your Name]".

Top 10 leads per scan

Never loses a scan

A flaky API call becomes a retry, not a lost scan. The whole pipeline runs as one durable workflow.

via Temporal

Every lead saved

Scores, signals and drafts are stored, so last week's scan is still there when you come back to it.

via MongoDB Atlas

Open-weight brain

Runs on open models through Groq, or fully on your own machine with Ollama. Switch with one setting.

No lock-in
MIT licensed

Open source. Take it, run it, sell it.

Every line is on GitHub. Self-host it for your own prospecting, or build a side business on top of it. The AI at its centre is open-weight, so a scan doesn't come with a per-token bill.
